The awards 賞

There are 10 awards for students and 6 for clubs. Each one has a single eligibility requirement the nominee must meet, plus criteria the selection committee uses to compare nominees.

Every criterion lists the proof we’ll ask for, so gather it before you fill out the form. A nomination doesn’t need to meet every criterion, but the more you can back up, the stronger it is.

Student awards 10

Trailblazer

For an AAPI student excelling in a field where AAPI people have historically been rare.

To be eligible: Holds an active role or roster spot in a field where AAPI people make up less than 15%.

What the committee looks for 4 criteria
  • Longevity. Took part for at least 2 consecutive seasons, production runs or terms. Proof: Official roster, playbill or appointment record
  • Leading role. Held a primary leading position. Proof: Performance credits, TSG cabinet listing or published articles
  • Outside recognition. Received an official athletic or conference honor, an artistic award, or a regional media feature. Proof: News link or award certificate
  • Historic first. The first, or one of fewer than 3 documented AAPI people, to hold this title or win this honor at Temple. Proof: Departmental verification or historical record

Cultural Ambassador

For a student who teaches others about heritage and cultural connection.

To be eligible: Directly organized cultural or heritage programming this academic year.

What the committee looks for 4 criteria
  • Sustained programming. Organized multiple cultural events across 2 or more semesters. Proof: Event dates or flyers
  • Educational content. Created structured educational programming, like a panel on cultural history, an identity or diaspora workshop, or a guest speaker. Proof: Slide deck, recording or program guide
  • Fundraising. Raised money for a cultural cause, charity or heritage initiative. Proof: Check, invoice or emails
  • Reach. Led a cultural event with more than 50 attendees. Proof: Proxy proof, like a sign-in sheet or event photos

Research

For high-level academic research with a peer-reviewed publication.

To be eligible: Has been part of a lab for at least 2 semesters. Proof: Email from your PI

What the committee looks for 5 criteria
  • First author. Listed as first author on a paper in an indexed, peer-reviewed journal or IEEE/ACM conference proceeding. Proof: The publication
  • Co-author. Listed as a contributing co-author on an indexed, peer-reviewed paper. Proof: The publication
  • Presentation. Gave an oral or poster presentation at a university, regional or national symposium or conference. Proof: Poster or presentation document
  • Research funding. Won a competitive research fellowship or grant, like CARAS, Diamond Research Scholars, MARC or NSF REU. Proof: Award letter
  • Academic standing. Cumulative GPA of 3.6 or higher. Proof: Transcript

Emerging Leader

For a freshman or sophomore already taking initiative in their community.

To be eligible: Is a first- or second-year undergraduate.

What the committee looks for 3 criteria
  • Official role. Holds an e-board seat, committee head or project director title in a student org or department. Proof: Suitable listing or Instagram board post
  • New initiative. Started and ran a new program, event or initiative that reached more than 20 students. Proof: Proxy proof, like a sign-in sheet or event photos
  • Balance. Holds an active leadership role with a cumulative GPA of 3.6 or higher. Proof: Transcript

Leadership

For a junior or senior who gives an organization direction and helps it grow.

To be eligible: Is a third-, fourth- or fifth-year undergraduate.

What the committee looks for 5 criteria
  • Official role. Holds an e-board seat, committee head or project director title in a student org or department. Proof: Suitable listing or Instagram board post
  • Team size. Led a team of more than 5 people.
  • Event scale. Led a major campus event with 75 or more attendees. Proof: Proxy proof, like a sign-in sheet or event photos
  • Track record. Moved up in leadership for at least 3 semesters in a row, for example committee member, then chair, then president. Proof: Instagram board posts
  • Measurable growth. Grew an organization by a clear margin, like raising $300, adding 15 active members or increasing attendance by 25%. Proof: Attendance comparison, budget sheet or Instagram analytics

First Generation Student

For a first-gen student reaching strong academic and professional milestones.

To be eligible: Neither parent or guardian completed a four-year bachelor’s degree in the United States.

What the committee looks for 5 criteria
  • GPA. Cumulative GPA above 3.5. Proof: Transcript
  • Working student. Works a part-time job while enrolled full time. Proof: Paystub or supervisor letter
  • Doing more. Also holds an e-board seat, a lab research position or a competitive internship. Proof: Roster or offer letter
  • Dean’s List. On the Dean’s List for at least 2 semesters. Proof: Transcript
  • Industry placement. Accepted a competitive internship, co-op or full-time offer at a Fortune 500 company, Big Tech, a top financial institution, a national lab or an established engineering firm. Proof: Offer letter

Business and Tech

For real results in entrepreneurship, technology, corporate finance or software engineering.

To be eligible: Main project or career focus is business, economics, technology, computer science or engineering.

What the committee looks for 4 criteria
  • Built something. Built and launched a working technical product. Proof: Live URL, GitHub repo, sales statement or demo
  • Competition. Placed 1st, 2nd or 3rd in a hackathon, case competition, startup incubator or other technical competition. Proof: Award certificate or press release
  • Industry role. Completed or accepted a competitive internship or position at an established or high-growth company. Proof: Offer letter
  • Career org leadership. Holds a leadership position in a career-related student society. Proof: Roster

Arts

For excellence in visual art, media, architecture, music, film or performance.

To be eligible: Majoring in or actively pursuing visual, performing, media or architectural arts, with a public portfolio.

What the committee looks for 5 criteria
  • Juried selection. Work selected for a juried or official exhibition, like a gallery show, film festival screening, or mainstage orchestra or theater production. Proof: Exhibition catalog, festival program, playbill or acceptance letter
  • Published work. Work published in an outside media outlet, architecture journal or literary review, or officially released (for example on Spotify or with an IMDb credit). Proof: Live link or publication scan
  • Paid work. Completed contracted creative, architectural or design work for paying clients or university departments. Proof: Invoices, or proof of the request and payment
  • Awards. Won a juried award, artistic grant or competition placement. Proof: Award certificate
  • Portfolio. Keeps a public portfolio with at least 5 finished works from the past 12 months. Proof: Website or portfolio link

Social Sciences

For measurable results in policy, human behavior, civic justice or education advocacy.

To be eligible: Academic or community focus in social sciences, education, legal studies or public policy.

What the committee looks for 3 criteria
  • Policy impact. Wrote or co-wrote a policy brief, draft legislation or institutional report that a government body, school board or university administration formally adopted or reviewed. Proof: Copy of the document or legislative record
  • Civic action. Organized a civic initiative, like a voter drive, labor campaign or mutual aid effort. Proof: Roster, campaign logs or press coverage
  • Research. Presented a social science capstone or thesis at an academic conference or symposium. Proof: Program schedule listing

Health Sciences

For real impact in clinical care, health equity or medical education.

To be eligible: Enrolled in a pre-health or health science program (CPH, CST Pre-Health, Pharmacy or Nursing).

What the committee looks for 5 criteria
  • Patient care. Documented clinical experience with direct patient interaction or observation, such as EMT, CNA, medical assistant, scribe, hospice or verified physician shadowing. Proof: Work log, timesheet or paystub
  • Service. Significant volunteer or community service hours in underserved communities. Proof: Signed volunteer hours
  • Research. Took part in laboratory, clinical or health sciences research. Proof: Peer-reviewed publication, accepted abstract or symposium presentation
  • Program admission. Verified offer to a graduate or professional health program (MD/DO, DMD/DDS, PharmD, PA, DPT, MSN, MPH or PhD). Proof: Offer letter
  • Exam scores. Scored in the 85th percentile or higher on the MCAT, DAT, OAT or GRE. Proof: Score report

Club awards 6

Most Philanthropic

For a club committed to community service, civic engagement and fundraising for causes beyond its own budget.

To be eligible: Completed at least one philanthropic initiative, charity fundraiser or group service project this academic year. Proof: Event flyer, project recap or donation receipt

What the committee looks for 5 criteria
  • Major donation. Raised and donated $500 or more to a nonprofit, disaster relief fund or community charity. Proof: Donation receipt or tax acknowledgement letter
  • Donation. Raised and donated $150 to $499 to a charitable cause. Proof: Donation receipt or tax acknowledgement letter
  • Group volunteering. Volunteered as a club in the local community, like at a soup kitchen, community garden or park cleanup. Proof: Volunteer coordinator letter, signed timesheet or attendance sheet
  • Community partnership. Set up a formal, ongoing volunteer relationship with an outside civic or nonprofit organization. Proof: Partnership agreement, co-hosted project flyer or coordinator email
  • Goods drive. Ran a drive that collected at least 50 items, like canned food, winter clothes, hygiene kits or school supplies. Proof: Drop-off receipt, inventory tally or handoff photo

Club of the Year

For a club showing excellence in every part of campus life.

To be eligible: Is a Registered Student Organization at Temple in good standing with Student Activities (not frozen or on probation). Proof: Suitable listing

What the committee looks for 6 criteria
  • Consistency. Hosted at least 6 separate meetings or events over the school year. Proof: Dated flyers, event logs or Instagram post history
  • Range. Hosted events in at least 2 categories: professional or academic, cultural, social, or community. Proof: Dated flyers, event logs or Instagram post history
  • Collaboration. Co-planned and co-hosted at least 2 events with other student orgs or university departments this year. Proof: Dated flyers, event logs or Instagram post history
  • Flagship event. Ran at least one major signature event for campus, like an annual banquet, conference, tournament, showcase or festival. Proof: Proxy proof, like a sign-in sheet or event photos
  • Turnout. Kept steady attendance (for example 30 or more students) across multiple meetings. Proof: Proxy proof, like a sign-in sheet or event photos
  • Self-funding. Raised $500 or more on its own through fundraising, merch, sponsors or alumni gifts. Proof: Budget sheets, transaction history, check or invoice

Best New Club

For a club founded in the last 1 to 2 years that has grown fast, built an active community and made a real contribution to campus.

To be eligible: Chartered, registered or reactivated with Temple Student Activities in Fall 2024 or later. Proof: Suitable listing or approval email

What the committee looks for 4 criteria
  • Membership. Has 25 or more active members in its first or second year. Proof: Proxy proof or membership fee records
  • Track record. Ran at least 3 standalone events or general body meetings this academic year. Proof: Event flyers, room confirmations or Instagram post history
  • Unmet need. Serves an identity, field or interest that wasn’t served on campus before. Proof: A one-paragraph mission statement and a sample event or workshop plan
  • Cross-club event. Co-hosted an event with an established student organization. Proof: Co-branded flyer or joint event listing

Most Improved Club

For a club that has turned itself around or changed significantly for the better.

To be eligible: Founded at least 2 years ago, and active both last year and this year. Proof: Suitable listing or Instagram

What the committee looks for 3 criteria
  • Membership growth. Grew active membership, meeting attendance or online community size compared with last year. Proof: Proxy proof, membership fee records or Instagram analytics
  • More programming. Increased the number or scope of events by at least 50% compared with last year. Proof: Last year’s and this year’s event calendars, or Instagram
  • Financial improvement. Has more money than last year, from fundraisers, sponsors or other sources. Proof: Budget sheets, transaction history, check or invoice

Advocacy and Social Justice

For a club leading on civil rights, equity, policy reform, cultural education or mental health awareness.

To be eligible: Organized, led or sponsored at least one campus initiative, workshop or campaign centered on social justice, civil rights, identity equity or systemic awareness this year. Proof: Event flyer, educational deck or public statement

What the committee looks for 4 criteria
  • Policy action. Wrote, sponsored or rallied support for a campus policy proposal, TSG resolution or open letter to the administration on equity, cultural resources or safety. Proof: Copy of the resolution, bill number or official letter
  • Education. Ran a workshop, panel or teach-in on systemic issues, like AAPI history, anti-Asian racism, mental health stigma or intersectionality. Proof: Slide deck, speaker bio or program guide
  • Public conversation. Produced an op-ed, awareness campaign or media feature that shaped public conversation, for example in The Temple News, a university newsletter or campus radio. Proof: Published link or scan
  • Media campaign. Made social media or print content raising awareness of systemic issues. Proof: Social posts, newsletter or print scans

Professionalism

For a club dedicated to career prep, industry readiness and mentorship, whose members land competitive opportunities.

To be eligible: Hosted at least one professional event, corporate info session or career workshop this academic year. Proof: Event flyers, Instagram history or event description

What the committee looks for 5 criteria
  • Recruiter access. Hosted an info session, networking event or site visit with outside recruiters or industry professionals. Proof: Co-branded flyer, emails with the speaker or event photos
  • Skill-building. Ran at least one hands-on career development workshop. Proof: Event flyer, emails with the speaker or event photos
  • Industry placement. A significant number of members or e-board accepted competitive internships, co-ops or full-time offers at Fortune 500 companies, Big Tech, top financial institutions, national labs or established engineering firms for the coming year. Proof: Redacted offer letters or LinkedIn announcements
  • Grad school. A significant number of members or e-board got offers from graduate or professional programs (master’s, PhD, medical, dental or law). Proof: Redacted offer letters or LinkedIn announcements
  • E-board academics. A significant number of e-board members are on the Dean’s List or have a cumulative GPA above 3.5. Proof: Transcripts or Dean’s List emails
